# What's new in Witchspace

Changes players will notice, newest first, in plain words. Commanders who've
played before get the newest section as a message from Witchspace when they next
dock (src/game/changelog.ts reads this file).

Adding to it: put a line under today's date with any change a player would
notice. Make a new heading for today if there isn't one, written like
`## 9 November 2026` (day, month in full, year). Write one line per change,
starting with `- `. Say what's different, not how it was done. Credit whoever
reported it.

## 11 November 2026

- Trading has a proper trade window, like a real exchange: Buy and Sell open it, with the order book beside the form, Price and Amount with + and −, a slider with stops, Total, and one button. Placing a limit order is no longer fiddly, on a phone or a computer. A limit buy fills your hold at most, unless you switch on Fill to storage, when the rest waits at the station. (Mark)
- All your orders in one place, at every market: open orders, goods waiting in storage and your trades, each with where it is and what your news says of it. Edit or cancel any of them. (Mark)
- Trade from the chart: SELL and BUY in its corner, a + on the price scale to place a limit order at that price (a long press on a phone), and your orders as tags on their lines that you can drag to a new price or cancel. You always confirm in the trade window. (Mark)
- Cancelling or changing an order at another market travels at the speed of news, as placing it did: it can still fill at its old price until the message gets there. (Mark)
- The ship's log tells you when a buy order fills, not only a sell. (Mark)
- A new rank with a company is its own message, however you earned it, and a notification if you've switched them on: "You're Contractor with Wexley-Yorozu now. Special orders will find you." (Mark)
- Choose what the flight recorder keeps: a switch for each kind of moment in the flight log (fights, your ship lost, Legends, the Marshals, crimes, trade runs, surveys). Surveys start off. Moments you keep by hand, and photos, always save. (A player on the ideas board)
- Letting moments go on a computer: an ✕ when you hover over one, and Select to let several go at once. (A player on the ideas board)
- Your record: on the Station screen, by your legal status. Each offence, when and where, what it made you, and the flight recorder's clip of it while that's still in your flight log; and how long until each system's record cools. (A player on the ideas board)
- The companies' special assignments reach your phone even on days you play: seeing one in the game no longer stops the notification. (Mark)

## 10 November 2026

- Busy systems are busy and lawless ones thin: at most three traders on the lanes in an anarchy, rising to eight in a corporate state. Traders who work the dangerous routes are skilled, with better guns that swing onto a target, and they fight back. (Mark)
- Shooting an escape pod is a serious crime, even a pirate's. The pod's beacon reports it, so it needs no witness: you're a Fugitive with a Co-operative Marshals warrant, and in an anarchy the Marshals issue the warrant anyway. (Mark)
- Renowned pirates carry a beam laser aft as well as forward: sitting on his tail is no longer safe. (Mark)
- The shipyard's Zaonce Rose is now Zaonce Neon, a hot raspberry, so nobody's Cobra passes for Odile Venn's pale rose. Ships already in Zaonce Rose have been repainted free. (Mark)
- On a computer the radio sits top left, as on a phone, with calls to answer under it. An important message lands in it as it shrinks away, rather than already being there. Whoever is in your sights is named just above them, not at the top edge. (Mark)
- Deleting your account asks whether to take your shared clips down too. Left up, they keep your callsign, and nobody else can claim it. (Mark)
- A renowned pirate no longer also sends the ordinary pirate's "Name your price" demand when he arrives. He speaks for himself, and he isn't for sale. (Mark)
- A deleted account is kept for 90 days, out of sight. Sign in again with the same email in that time and it all comes back: callsign, cloud save, card and firsts. After that it's erased. (Mark)

## 9 November 2026

- Renowned pirates remember every commander. Put one in his pod and he's out of action for everyone, and the bar knows who did it. When he wins, he tells the next commander. Signed-in commanders only.
- Renowned pirates in the saloon. Beat one and he's in his home saloon with his arm in a sling. Lose to him and he's buying the rounds, with your ship's name chalked behind the bar. The galaxy chart says whose patch a system is, and the news carries his story. (Mark)
- Renowned pirates. Most lawless systems have one man the locals talk about, flying a good Cobra. Keep killing his people there and he comes for you himself. He fights from range, hits harder, and pulls out to recharge. You get a warning and about 90 seconds to run. Beat him and he ejects, and he'll remember. (Mark)
- Important radio messages show for a moment in the middle of the screen, then shrink away into the radio's corner, where they're kept. (Mark)
- The trip computer says what the next leg needs when you arrive: where the next jump goes, how far, and whether to refuel first.
- Trails are said plainly. A trail step's signal is named "Trail signal" in gold. Finding one says which trail, how many steps you've found and where it leads next. The galaxy chart marks each trail's next system and lists your trails. (Mark)
- Finding a trail step no longer replaces a course you've already set, for a contract say. It tells you where the trail goes instead.
- Every signal's log is kept in your messages, to read again when you're docked. (a player on the ideas board)
- The job card and the course you plot now take the same route. The card could say one system while the course went to another, which sent you round in circles. (Mark)
- Running dark past the police now gets one hail and one countdown. If the Viper that hailed you docks, the next one in sight carries the hail on, instead of starting a new 15 seconds.
- The jump countdown names where you're going.
- Routes are planned as far as your tank reaches, not a fixed 7 light years.
- The local chart shows your whole range on a wide screen, and draws your next jump.
- "Find a system" no longer covers the chart's title on a phone held sideways.
- The docking computer waits while the station's rings are red, and no longer flips between holding and going in at a busy station.
- Police and militia never launch out of a slot you've been cleared into: they start from the far side of the station instead.
- Answers to a police hail are easier to see and tap on a phone.
- The market reads "computers are" and "food is".
