Short

Witchspace is a free, open-source space trading and combat game that runs in the browser: a love letter to Elite (1984). Fly a Cobra Mk III across eight galaxies of glowing vector space, trade on living markets, fight pirates, dodge the law, and switch to an authentic 1984 look with one key.

Longer

In 1984 Ian Bell and David Braben fitted eight galaxies, wireframe space flight and an open-ended trading game into about 22 KB of a BBC Micro, and invented a genre. Witchspace is a fan's tribute: an open-source game that keeps the soul of the original (the crisp vector ships, the galaxy you remember, that heart-in-mouth docking) and builds the parts a 1984 computer never could around it.

It runs in any browser. The neo-vector look brings glowing lines and shattering explosions; one key drops you back to 1984. The markets are a real exchange, with prices moved by NPC traders, supply chains and world events, and news that travels at the speed of ships, so the commander who hears first can profit. Break the law badly enough and the Co-operative Marshals come for you through a witchspace rift. Everything is designed in the open, and the community can add ships, worlds, events and missions as data files.

What's in it

  • Eight galaxies of 2,048 star systems, generated just where they were in 1984
  • Flight with the original's feel: pitch, roll and no drift, with the docking computer and its waltz
  • Combat against pirates and police, with lasers on four mounts, missiles, ECM and a combat rating from Harmless to Elite
  • Living markets as a trading exchange: candlestick charts, an order book, limit orders, NPC traders who move prices and supply chains
  • World events (famines, strikes, wars, blockades) and news that travels at the speed of ships
  • The law: local records, police scans, fines, surrender, and a black market where the stricter the law, the better it pays
  • The Co-operative Marshals: commit a serious crime and their carrier tears its way in through a witchspace rift
  • Fuel scoops: skim a star for fuel and scoop cargo from wrecks
  • A 1984 mode, one keypress away: white lines on black, sharpened for modern screens
  • Photos: press P for a clean shot of the view without the HUD, saved or shared straight from your phone
  • Classic rules (one life, as in 1984) or Modern rules (insurance, flight assists)

About the name and the tribute

Witchspace is an unofficial, non-commercial tribute to Elite (1984) by Ian Bell and David Braben. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Frontier Developments, Ian Bell or David Braben. Elite is a trademark of Frontier Developments plc. The ship names are a deliberate tribute; the code, ship designs, text and art were made from scratch for this project.
