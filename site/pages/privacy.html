Privacy

Witchspace keeps as little about you as it can. No adverts, no tracking cookies, and nothing is sold.

Playing without an account

Your commander is saved in your own browser and never leaves it. Nothing about you is sent to us.

If you sign in

  • What we keep: your email address, your callsign, and a copy of your commander (the cloud save), so you can carry on from any device.
  • Why: your email is only used to send you sign-in links. We don't send newsletters or anything else unless you ask for them.
  • If you register your ship: shared space needs a registered ship. Registering is free and for Modern rules only (Classic stays as it was in 1984), and solo play doesn't need it. When you register, we keep these with your account, to keep shared space fair: her transponder number (such as WS-7F3K-29); when and at which port you registered; the ship as she was then (hull, lasers, kit, missiles, credits and rank, and whether she was a basic Cobra); and where your trade ledger stood. Each hull also has its own log (registered, and later kit fitted, traded in or destroyed), which holds only her type and number, never your account or callsign.
  • If you let the companies contact you: only if you switch it on (press A, then Let the companies contact me). For notifications we keep the address each browser you switch them on in gives us, which identifies nothing but that browser; for email we use your sign-in address. Messages come at most twice a week, in character, when a special assignment is waiting, and every email has a one-click stop. Switch it off at any time, and deleting your account removes it.
  • Security: sign-in links (and the 6-digit codes sent with them) work once and expire after 20 minutes; a code allows five tries. Links, codes and sessions are stored only as one-way hashes. You stay signed in on a device for 30 days from your last visit. To save typing, your browser remembers the last email you signed in with, on that device only.
  • What's public by your callsign: being first to bring a body home (your firsts, on the galaxy chart and the home page's live feed); beating, losing to or running from a renowned pirate, on his record for 60 days; and clips you share, whose page says "CMDR" and your callsign, which also labels your ship in the recording. A clip stays up until you take it down from your flight log, or it's removed after reports. Once a clip is down, its video and picture are erased after 30 days. Other commanders in your clips are never named. As a guest nothing is shown by name: your clips say "A commander", and guests can't claim firsts.
  • Deleting it: press A on the title screen, then Delete account. Straight away, you're signed out everywhere, emails and notifications stop (your sessions and contact settings are deleted), and your commander card, firsts, pirate record and ship's registration are taken out of view. The rest (your email, callsign, cloud save, card, firsts, pirate record, ship's registration and submissions) is kept out of sight for 90 days, in case you change your mind: sign in again with the same email in that time and it all comes back. After 90 days it's erased for good. Each hull's own log stays, as it holds only her type and number. The same screen lets you take down the clips you've shared; it's your choice, and the box starts unticked. Clips you leave up keep your callsign in their title and recording, so the callsign stays held and nobody else can take it. With no clips left up, your callsign is freed after the 90 days. To have a clip taken down later, email hello@witchspace.org.uk. Your commander stays in your browser.

Shared space

Shared space needs a signed-in commander with a registered ship (see If you register your ship, above). When you fly in shared space, your ship's position, callsign and rank are sent to the other commanders in your star system while you're there. Nothing is kept after you leave.

The ideas board

To give each idea one vote per person without accounts, your browser keeps a random id. To stop abuse we keep a one-way hash of your network address, never the address itself. What you post is public.

Counting how it's played

Once a day, the game tells us which rules your commander uses (Modern or Classic), whether the 1984 look is on, and whether it's a phone, tablet or computer, and when a new commander is started, which rules they chose. The game works out the kind of device from the screen; for older copies of the game, the server reads your browser's description of itself to tell, and doesn't keep it. We keep only the day's totals, and the number of commanders who flew in shared space each day (to count each one once, it remembers which accounts it has counted for a day or so, then forgets). To count each device once a day we keep a one-way hash of your network address for that day, never the address itself, and nothing else about you.

Counting visits

We count visits to the website and the game with Vercel Web Analytics: which pages are seen, which site sent you there, and roughly which country and kind of device you're on. It uses no cookies and keeps nothing that identifies you: a visitor is told apart by a hash that changes every day, and your network address isn't kept.

Who handles it

The website is hosted by Vercel, which also counts the visits. Shared space, the ideas board and accounts run on Cloudflare. Sign-in emails, and company messages if you ask for them, are sent by Resend. Notifications go through your browser's own push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla), which sees only that a message arrived, not what it says. Images and videos are served by Cloudinary. A film that lives on YouTube plays in YouTube’s privacy-enhanced player (youtube-nocookie.com), which loads from YouTube with the page and sets no cookies until you press play. Cloudflare's Turnstile checks that you're a person when you sign in or post an idea. The website's fonts come from Google Fonts, which sees your browser's address when it loads them.
