Shared space

The other ship on your scanner might be a person. In shared space you fly in the same star system as other commanders, live: you see their ships, read their callsigns and ranks, and choose whether to fly together, keep your distance or fight.

How to join

One key, on the title screen

  1. Open the game at witchspace.org.uk/play, on a computer or a phone. Nothing to install.
  2. Press S on the title screen, or tap Shared space (beta), until it says on. It stays on until you turn it off.
  3. Fly under Modern rules. Shared space is Modern only; Classic stays the 1984 game, alone.
  4. Launch. As you leave the station the game tells you who else is in the system: “Shared space · 2 other commanders in Lave”. Hyperspace takes you to the next system’s, and whoever is there.

Want to meet someone? Pick a system and a time, and both launch there. Lave, where every commander starts, is a good place to look.

What you’ll see

Every commander is a person, and you’ll know which

Their ship, in their paint

Other commanders fly the ships they own, in the colours they chose at the yard: a Cobra in Diso Amber is somebody’s Cobra.

A callsign and a rank

A tag follows each one: their rank insignia, callsign and range, like ◆◆ CMDR Jameson · 0.8 km. Signed-out players show as guests.

A diamond on the scanner

Commanders are diamonds; the ships the game flies are short bars. A commander who has just fired on you turns red, and stays red for a while.

Ships running dark

Switch your transponder off (0) and you’re an unidentified ship until someone gets close enough to read your hull. The police will want a word.

Fair play

The server referees

Each star system has its own small server, and it decides what happened, not your browser.

  • Flying: it refuses impossible moves (too fast, teleporting, outside the system) and puts you back where you were.
  • Lasers: it checks the laser is fitted and not firing faster than it can, then judges the shot where the shooter saw the target. It keeps everyone’s shields and hull, and only the server says who was destroyed.
  • Missiles and ECM: lock on (T), launch (X). The server flies the missile and decides if it hits. An ECM destroys every missile nearby, yours included.
  • Collisions: both ships take damage, more the faster they met. A collision is an accident: no crime, no kill.
  • The law: firing on a commander makes you an Offender. Destroying one brings a Marshals warrant, anywhere there’s law. Anarchies have none: fly there at your own risk.

Your callsign

Sign in, and it’s yours alone

A callsign nobody else has

Sign in with your email (no password: we send a code) and your callsign belongs to your account. Without an account you fly as a guest.

One ship per account

Join from a second window or device and the first one leaves shared space, so nobody meets themselves or flies two ships at once.

The same commander everywhere

Your commander is saved to your account, so the phone and the computer carry on from each other.

Even when you fly alone

One galaxy, the same for everyone

With shared space off, you don’t see other ships, but you still live in the same galaxy as every other commander.

  • One clock: the news, the markets, the contracts and the shipyards’ build lines are worked out from the galaxy’s own clock, so a famine at Riedquat is a famine for everyone.
  • Firsts: the first commander to survey a world or finish a trail goes on the chart, and on the home page, for everyone to see.
  • Numbered editions: when the yard says 41 of 100 are left, that’s 41 for the whole galaxy.
