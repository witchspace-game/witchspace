# Witchspace worldbuilding bible

*A living document. Propose changes by pull request.*

## Tone

Lonely, wry and a little lawless. Space is enormous and indifferent; commerce is what holds it together. Humour is dry, never slapstick. Think frontier town with hyperspace: traders, bounty hunters, bored police and things in the dark between the stars.

Write flavour text like a terse ship's manual or a bar-room rumour, not a fantasy novel. One or two sentences; let the player imagine the rest.

## The setting

- **Eight galaxies**, each with 256 star systems. Jumps within a galaxy go through **witchspace**, a hyperspace transit that is fast, mostly safe and occasionally *not* safe.
- **Coriolis stations** orbit most inhabited worlds and are the hubs of trade and law.
- **Governments** range from Corporate States and Democracies down to Feudal worlds, Dictatorships and outright Anarchies. The lower the order, the higher the profit and the risk.
- **Economies** run from Rich Industrial to Poor Agricultural. Goods flow from where they're made to where they're needed.
- **Thargoids** are an insectoid alien threat that can drag ships out of witchspace mid-jump. Nobody quite knows what they want.
- **Police** fly with beacons, a hull band and a transponder issued and keyed by the Galactic Co-operative. None of these can be bought, copied or painted on. A second-hand Viper is just a Viper, and no station, however corrupt, will mistake it for the law.
- **Commanders** are ranked from *Harmless* to *Elite* by combat record. Legal status is *Clean*, *Offender* or *Fugitive*.

## Naming

- **Star systems:** short, alien-sounding names of 2–4 syllables built from simple syllable pairs (e.g. *Tibedi*, *Orerve*, *Anlama*). Avoid real places, real people and existing game systems.
- **Ships:** original *Elite* class names are used as a tribute; new classes should feel like they belong (snake/animal names, "Mk" variants, blunt industrial names).
- **Manufacturers:** slightly old-fashioned company names, often two surnames.

## Content we'd love

- New ship classes that fill a gap (heavy freighters, mining tugs, courier boats).
- Hand-crafted landmark systems with a hook: a rumour, a hazard or an opportunity.
- Missions with a moral wrinkle.
