# Design: combat computer routines

*Proposed, October 2026, from Mark: "I am sure we can find advanced flight routines and upgrades." Change by pull request.*

The combat computer is in the game (Modern rules, 6,000 Cr at tech level 9 and up). Press **O** and it flies your ship in a fight while you work a rear or side laser, the turrets. It flies one way, a textbook chase: close in, pass, break away, come round. That's predictable on purpose, so a good pilot beats it, and kills made with it on count half towards your combat rating.

**Now cleverer** *(Mark, 30 October 2026: "It should be properly clever and rotate through proper evasive flying techniques and know who or what is attacking the ship!"; built)*. It watches every hostile: who's on your tail, whose nose is on you, who just hit you. It turns on whoever is hurting you, not just the ship in front, and picks a manoeuvre for the moment:
- **Attack run:** the chase, as before.
- **Break turn:** a sustained hard pull at full speed when someone is on your tail, so the line between you swings faster than they can follow.
- **Scissors:** hard reversals when they're close behind, until they overshoot.
- **Jinking:** a corkscrew when you're under fire.
- **Extending:** away from everyone to let the shields come back or the laser cool, then back in.

It never answers a tail with the same move twice running, and it says what it's doing and why on the HUD ("Break turn · Mamba firing on you"). In tests, a chasing pirate has you in its sights for about a fifth as long as when you fly straight (break turn), and under half with scissors. The gimballed front laser tracks the computer's target while it flies (Mark: the ring was "all over the place"). Kills with it on still count half, and a good commander still flies better. The routines below build on this: a dearer computer could fly them better, and aces still learn them.

One habit is thin. Against the Falcon, who sits on your tail and [reads how you fly](legends-the-falcon.md#how-she-fights-the-ambusher), it's a liability. This design makes the computer a kit you choose: routines you buy and fit, better hardware, and pilots who learn to answer both.

## Principles

- **Every routine has a counter.** A routine is a pattern, and a pattern can be read. Nothing you buy wins a fight by itself.
- **Your own flying stays best.** No mark of computer flies better than a good commander, and kills with it on still count half. The best commanders fly by hand and give the computer one job.
- **Routines suit kit.** Most need the right lasers to be worth fitting: a reason to think about your whole ship.
- **Readable.** You can see what your computer is doing (its routine is named on the HUD), and so can the pilot you're fighting.
- **Modern only.** Classic has no combat computer, as in 1984.

## The routines

Sold at stations like equipment, the dearer ones at higher tech levels. Fit as many as you like; the computer picks the one that suits the moment, and you can lock it to one.

| Routine | What it flies | Wants | Its counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Textbook chase** (comes with it) | Close in, pass, break away, come round | Any turret | Time your run for its break, when its tail turns to you at a known angle |
| **Tail guard** | Something behind you: keep it in the rear laser's arc, weaving so it can't settle | A rear laser | Come from below or off the quarter, where the rear laser has to chase you too |
| **Scissors** | Turn into a pursuer again and again until it overshoots in front of you | Front laser, a tight-turning hull | Hang back and wait for it to stop turning |
| **Hit and run** | A fast pass, then open the range so your lasers cool and your shields recharge | Beam lasers, which overheat | Follow it out: it's running, so it isn't shooting |
| **Broadside** | Hold a target off your beam, side lasers on it | Side lasers | Stay on its nose or tail, where side lasers can't reach |
| **Evasive weave** | Stop fighting and survive: weave for the station or the jump | Nothing; for traders | Cut it off from where it's heading |

The anti-Falcon kit is a rear laser and Tail guard; the Falcon's answer to it is the rear quarter (see [a reason for a rear laser](legends-the-falcon.md#how-she-fights-the-ambusher)).

## The hardware: Marks I to III

| Mark | Reacts | Reads your target's turn | Switches routine | Price (to balance) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** (today's) | Slowly | Not at all: it chases where the target is | Never: one routine at a time, your choice | 6,000 Cr |
| **II** | Quicker | A little ahead | Between fitted routines, a beat late | about 15,000 Cr |
| **III** | Close to a good human | Well ahead | Cleanly, at the right moment | about 40,000 Cr, high-tech worlds only |

A Mark III is close to a good commander and never better than your own best flying.

## Who reads it

Pilots learn routines the way people do, by seeing them ([pilot skill](pilot-skill.md)):

- **Rookies** fall for any routine.
- **Aces** learn each routine's pattern. Scissors works once on an ace; the second time it hangs back and waits for you to stop turning. A higher mark hides the pattern a little longer.
- **The Falcon reads your kit** as she comes in dark: Tail guard fitted, and she comes from below. Beat her with it, and she [remembers](legends-the-falcon.md#she-remembers-you).

## Building it in stages

| Stage | What |
|---|---|
| **1** | Routines as equipment: Tail guard and Hit and run first (they answer the Falcon and the beam laser's heat), then Scissors, Broadside and Evasive weave. The routine named on the HUD; lock to one with a second press of O. |
| **2** | Marks II and III, the computer choosing between fitted routines. |
| **3** | Aces and the Falcon reading your routines, with [pilot skill](pilot-skill.md) stage 3. |

## Open questions

- Should routines be equipment (one slot each), or software upgrades to the one computer, bought once and kept?
- Should a fight flown mostly by hand, with the computer used for a moment, still count half, or only the kills made while it flew?
- Could commanders write and share their own routines, a community contribution like ships and Titanoboa modules, tested against the same duels?
