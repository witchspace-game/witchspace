# Exploration

Explore should be as good as Trade and Fight. Trading has contracts, an exchange and four companies; fighting has the law, the Marshals and the police hail. A commander who'd rather wander needs the same: **a reason to go somewhere, something worth finding when you get there, things to spend on, and something to show for it.**

This builds on the first slice (rings, moons, surveys, signals; see [ideas](../ideas.md#exploration)) and ties exploring into the companies ([companies and contracts](companies-and-contracts.md)), above all **Wexley-Yorozu**, the terraformers, whose Science Division pays best for what explorers find ([lore](../companies.md)).

## Principles

- **Seeded, the same for everyone.** Worlds, moons, signals, trails and their stories come from the galaxy's seed: every commander finds the same derelict at the same place, alone or in shared space. That costs nothing to run and lets players compare notes.
- **Modern rules pay.** Classic stays as it was: rings, moons and the visited record only.
- **Grows with use.** The shared galaxy adds names (first discoveries, colony charters) as a few records on the server, not a simulation.
- **Small stories, our own words.** Logs are short, specific and ours: no original prose, no real names.
- **Every trail is somebody's story.** One person at its heart, told only in what they left behind, and a feeling that turns along the way: **glad, sad, mad, bad**. Nobody remembers a clause; they remember the girl whose message was never sent. The company plots are the skeleton; the people are what make them real.

## What's there (the first slice)

- **Worlds:** rings on about one planet in eight, up to two moons.
- **Surveys:** hold low over the planet, close to a moon or hot by the star; the data sells at any station, half price where it was gathered and up to five times far away.
- **Signals:** derelicts, beacons and wreck fields with written logs and salvage, picked up by a planet survey or by flying near.
- **The record:** the chart rings systems you've visited; explorer points and a rank from Homebody to Voyager. *(Elite is the combat rank only: nobody reaches it by exploring.)*

## Stage 1: reasons to go, things to spend on *(built, October 2026)*

### Trails

A derelict's log points somewhere. Follow it and the next find continues the story, usually **three to five systems** long, each within one ordinary jump of the last, ending in something worth the trip.

- **Sixteen trails a galaxy,** placed from the seed. The first find of each is an ordinary-looking signal in its starting system; each later one appears only once you've found the one before (it's what the clue told you to look for), already marked on your scanner when you arrive.
- **Finding a step** plots your course to the next system and adds the log to your survey data (it sells like any other). The **end** pays: a cache of salvage, valuable data and 25 explorer points.
- **Four stories to start:**
  - *The colony that stopped answering* (Wexley-Yorozu). A relay beacon still repeating "all well" eleven years on, and behind it in the queue a message never sent: nine-year-old **Mira** asking her gran whether the yellow flowers came up. The tug *Hold Fast*, cable cut, forty bunks rigged in the hold: pilot **Sam Dray** was told not to answer the Ninefold's call, and answered. The survey tender *Patience*, whose agronomist warned that the seed was never tested. A quarantine buoy asking ships not to render assistance, rewired by someone to give the course anyway. And the colony ship *Ninefold* in a parking orbit, lights on, air good, two hundred bunks made, nobody aboard; on the bridge, the report, and a note from the administrator, **Teodor Vasse**: "Read it. Then decide who you give it to."
  - *The long way home.* **Ada**'s letters to **Noor**, one per system, after nine years away: glad to be coming home, sad when her escort's drive gives out, done being angry about why she left. Her ship is found one jump short, out of fuel, the escape pod gone and aimed at home, and a last letter: sell what's in the hold, send Noor the money, tell her I was nearly there.
  - *The lost survey.* **O. Fenn**, chartmaker, racing her failing eyesight: "The doctors give me a year of good sight. Forty systems left. I can do forty." She turns down Wexley-Yorozu's offer for the unfinished chart ("it goes to everyone or no one"), and the last beacon has the full chart, the compass rose complete, its last point drawn big and crooked: "Done. For anyone who flies."
  - *A debt unpaid.* What looks like a pirate's kill tally (eleven marks, some crossed through) is **Bel**'s: the twelfth hauler of a convoy whose route was sold for 300 Cr. She is crossing off the ones who sold them. Her cache holds the guns and narcotics she took off them, and eleven envelopes for the families, to go to the Mutual.
- **The Ninefold report: the explorer's choice.** Read it first (**Read the Ninefold report** at any station): the agronomist's warning, Regional Director **Corwin Ashe**'s memo (relief would cost 310,000 Cr, recovery under clause 214 only 40,000; keep the relay running so the families aren't alarmed), Vasse's plea from the sealed bridge, the recovery count (158 taken for yard work, 41 missing with Sam Dray, Vasse taken last, having stayed so the tug could leave), and Mira's message. Then choose:
  - **Hand it to Wexley-Yorozu** (any W-Y station): the Science Division pays **1,200 Cr** and remembers you kindly (+3 standing). The relay keeps saying all well, and they go looking for Sam Dray.
  - **Publish it** anywhere else: the Mutual's newsletter pays **300 Cr**, the families learn what happened, Mira's message reaches her gran, and Wexley-Yorozu remembers you differently (−3).
  - It doesn't sell with the rest of your data. The game records which you chose, for later: where Sam Dray and the forty went, and what Corwin Ashe does next.
- **On the Station tab:** trails under way, how far along, and where the last clue pointed.

### Terraforming candidates

Every system's planet is already home to someone, so Wexley-Yorozu looks at the **moons**. A moon survey now grades it: **Barren** (about half), **Marginal** (a third) or **Candidate** (about one in six; bigger moons more often). A Candidate's data is worth **four times** as much: it's the start of a colony.

### Wexley-Yorozu: the best price for data, and survey contracts

- **At any station where Wexley-Yorozu works, survey data sells for a quarter more.**
- **Survey contracts** on the Contracts tab at W-Y stations: *survey the moons at Riedquat, data to any Wexley-Yorozu station within so many jumps.* No cargo, so any ship can take one; paid like a contract, with standing. When you hand it in, W-Y keeps the moon data (it's theirs now), so it isn't paid twice. Not lost if your ship is.

### Explorer's kit

- **Long-Range Fuel Tanks** (2,200 Cr, tech 8): ten light years in the tank instead of seven. Structural: they can't be knocked out.
- **Deep Survey Scanner** (1,800 Cr, tech 7): surveys from twice as far and in half the time, and picks up signals from four times as far.

Both Modern only.

## Stage 1b: the explorer's road *(built, October 2026)*

Exploring paid, but it didn't lead anywhere: the rank was only a word on the Station tab. Trading buys a better ship; fighting climbs to Elite and the Marshals. Now the explorer's rank **opens doors**, and what you find **carries your name**. (Elite stays the combat rank: nobody reaches it by exploring.)

### The rank opens doors

| Rank | Points | What opens |
|---|---|---|
| Pathfinder | 100 | **Science Division special orders** at Wexley-Yorozu stations: surveys of systems with a terraforming Candidate, paid three quarters more than an ordinary survey. And the **Survey Autopilot** goes on sale. |
| Trailblazer | 250 | **Trail tip-offs:** the Station tab names the nearest trail you haven't started, and where its first signal is. And the **lost courier** is out there to find (below). |
| Pioneer | 600 | The **Route Autopilot** goes on sale. |
| Voyager | 1,500 | The Legends, when they're built (stage 4): the first invitation goes to Voyagers. |

Reaching a rank says what it opened, there and then.

### Advanced autopilots

The explorer's work is long flights and patient holding. The autopilots do the flying; you choose where.

- **Survey Autopilot** (3,000 Cr, tech 8, Pathfinder and above). Press **9** (or the **Survey** button). It picks the nearest thing in the system still to survey or find (the planet, a moon, a signal you've detected), flies there, using in-system jumps when it can, steering wide of the planet, and holds you in survey range until it's done, then moves on to the next. It leaves the star to you: no autopilot flies into that heat. Any stick or throttle input, or 9 again, hands control back. A hostile ship coming close, damage, or nothing left to do also hands it back, but it brings the ship to a stop first: it never leaves you coasting into a moon.
- **Route Autopilot** (5,000 Cr, tech 10, Pioneer and above). With a course plotted, it starts the next jump by itself about twelve seconds after you arrive in each system on the way, as long as there's fuel and nothing hostile nearby. Press H during the countdown to stop it. It never lands you anywhere you didn't plot.

Both Modern only, and both can be knocked out in a fight like any other equipment.

### The lost courier and the Will-o'-the-Wisp

Somewhere in each galaxy a Science Division courier, the *Quiet Hand*, drifts with no crew and its bay sealed from the inside. In the rack is a prototype stamped **WILL-O'-THE-WISP**: the marsh light you chase and never catch. Its last order: "Under no circumstances let this fall into private hands."

- **Found, never bought.** One courier a galaxy, placed from the seed. Its signal shows only to Trailblazers and up, found like any other (survey the planet, then fly to it). Within 20 light years, the Station tab carries **bar talk** of it ("a Science Division courier went dark within 15 light years of here"); in its system, "survey the planet and listen".
- **What it does** (Modern): it jinks the ship out of the way, so **enemy lasers miss half the time**, and **half the missiles fired at you are spoofed** as they close. It can't be knocked out, and nobody will buy it from you. Hard to shoot down, not impossible: shields, hull and good flying still matter.
- Finding it is worth 25 explorer points. Wexley-Yorozu will want it back (a thread for later).

### Your name on the galaxy

When you **sell or hand in data at a station while signed in** with a callsign, the shared galaxy records who got there first: one record per world, moon and signal, the first commander to bring its data home.

- **The chart** shows it: *Charted first by CMDR Nova* for a system's planet, *Candidate charter: CMDR Nova* for a terraforming Candidate (your name on the colony charter, for stage 2), *Found first by …* for signals and trail steps.
- **The Station tab** counts your firsts.
- First come, first named: whoever reaches a station with the data first. Not signed in? The game works as before, and nobody's name goes down.
- It costs one small record per body on the server (a table in the accounts object), read a galaxy at a time and cached. The client says what it surveyed and the server takes its word, as it does for saves; the claims are checked for shape and sense (real systems, body names the game uses, no more than a hold's worth at once).

## Stage 2: worlds you can watch change

**Finds that change the world:** precious deposits, found by explorers and sold to a company, that become developments every career supplies, guards or raids. Designed in [from discovery to development](discovery-to-development.md).

Alongside the station yards of [building worlds](companies-and-contracts.md#4b-building-worlds):

- **Worlds being terraformed.** A few systems with thin populations have Wexley-Yorozu processors at work: a point of interest in orbit, and a planet whose sky shifts colour over real weeks, driven by the clock so everyone sees the same stage. Survey the progress for W-Y.
- **Candidates become colonies.** The first commander to hand in a Candidate's data already has their callsign on its charter (stage 1b); here the colony is founded and grows.
- **The darker side.** W-Y wants Candidates before anyone files a claim. Sell a find to a rival, or warn the people who'd be moved off: the explorer's own moral choices.

## Stage 3: witchspace itself

- **Misjumps.** Rarely, a jump drops you in deep space between systems, with a wreck field or a signal and little fuel. Danger and drama, and the door to Thargoid space later.
- **Rifts** like the Marshals' that open on their own and lead somewhere unmarked.

## Stage 4: Legends

Very rare seeded encounters at the end of the longest trails. The first: the original game's first mission, the hunt for a stolen prototype, the **Constrictor**, as a trail of sightings across a galaxy.

## Later: the shared galaxy and community content

- **A photo gallery** of the best P-key shots, by system.
- **Community content:** trails, logs and Legends as data files with a validator, like ships, so anyone can add a place worth finding.
